# Minimum Viable Intervention

What is one change we could make to an existing process that is likely to produce a net-positive step toward a community's stated objectives with as little disruption as possible?

Santa Rosa already has a social water acquisition system that functions well enough to support the community. But only having running water 3 hours a day, 2 days a week makes life hard. The water is there, it's just a matter of producing the energy required to pump it to the surface.

Community leadership has stated that their goal is to reach free 24/7 access to running water for the community. A system created to achieve this goal might take many forms. Weighing the relative benefits of (ie.) a permanent diesel generator vs a solar battery system requires knowledge about the community and its social context.

Rather than implementing a complete solution based on the desired outcome, a minimum viable intervention approach starts with a system that already works and takes one step to move that existing system toward the desired outcome. This approach takes time but it should preserve existing power structures within the community and give the community more control over the outcome as they will have an opportunity to steer the process after each step. This approach also creates time for the supporting organization to build trust in the community and learn about the social context.

Every 3 months, progress will be reviewed and new interventions proposed.
